# History and research

OpenSync began with research into acoustic matching and developed into a Go engine, native project format, Premiere exporter, and Windows workstation. Python research prototypes remain in the repository as historical and development material. Public synchronization, export, and import-audit paths migrated to Go, with FFmpeg and FFprobe as external tools.

The difficult C0559 and 2144 investigation found that distributed temporal sections and complementary frequency bands could expose a promising candidate missed by whole-recording ranking. Its independent short holdout was inconclusive. The result is a research clue and a hard-positive discovery case, with exact independent certification still open.

Large concert recordings prompted investigation of microphone distance, propagation, reflections, clock drift, and weak pair evidence. Chronological placement grew as a separate source of evidence for continuous events. Manual review added reversible human decisions for competing candidates.

Current experiments include an opt-in shared FFmpeg library decoder and performance work on large projects. Treat dated design documents marked proposed, undecided, or unimplemented as historical context. Check current code, tests, and the README for present behavior.
